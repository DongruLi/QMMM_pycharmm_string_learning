# Understand the String Method

The **string method in collective variables (CVs)** is a free-energy sampling method used to determine a **minimum free energy path (MFEP)** connecting two states of a molecular system.

For a chemical reaction, these two states can be the **reactant** and **product**. Instead of describing the reaction using a single structure, the string method represents the reaction pathway using a series of intermediate points called **images**.

Each image describes one position along the reaction pathway in collective-variable space.

---

## 1. Why do we need a reaction path?

A chemical reaction in a molecular simulation involves changes in many atomic coordinates.

For example, a reaction may involve:

- breaking a chemical bond,
- forming a new chemical bond,
- rearrangement of the substrate,
- and changes in the surrounding protein and solvent environment.

Following all Cartesian coordinates directly would make the reaction pathway difficult to describe.

Instead, we select a small number of **collective variables (CVs)** that describe the most important structural changes during the reaction.

For a reaction involving one breaking bond and one forming bond, two useful CVs may be

$$
\xi_1 = r_{\mathrm{breaking}}
$$

and

$$
\xi_2 = r_{\mathrm{forming}}
$$
